From one file to one clip

Trim a track

  1. Load your file. Drop an audio file on the box, or click it to browse. MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG and FLAC work in most browsers.
  2. Mark what to keep. Drag the two amber handles on the waveform. The shaded area between IN and OUT is the part you’ll keep.
  3. Listen to it. Press Play selection (or the space bar). Turn on Loop to hear the section repeat while you adjust the handles.
  4. Choose a format. WAV keeps full quality. MP3 makes a smaller file. Tick Fade in / out to soften the start and end over 150 ms.
  5. Export. Select Export trimmed audio. Your browser saves the clip, named like song-trimmed.wav, to your downloads.

Need to be more precise? Drag the Zoom slider to stretch the waveform, and use the thin strip underneath to scroll along it.

Use a video’s sound

  1. Add the video. Drop an MP4, MOV or WEBM file on the box, or click it to browse.
  2. Wait for the audio. Some videos open straight away. For others, the video plays silently in the background while its sound is recorded, which takes about as long as the video. A percentage shows the progress. Keep this tab open until it finishes.
  3. Trim as usual. Once the waveform appears, set IN and OUT exactly as you would for any audio file.
  4. Export. Save the clip as WAV or MP3. The video itself isn’t changed or exported.

Tip: A shorter video is much quicker. If you only need part of a long video, trim it in a video app first, then bring the shorter file here.

To the millisecond

Set exact times

  1. Type a time. Click the IN or OUT readout and type, for example 01:23.500. Press Enter to apply or Esc to cancel.
  2. Edit one part. Click just the minutes, seconds or milliseconds to change only that part. ↑ and ↓ change it by one, and ← and → move between parts.
  3. Nudge a handle. Press Tab to focus a handle, then use ← and → to move it 10 ms. Hold Shift to move it 500 ms.
  4. Mark from the playhead. Click the waveform (or play it) to place the playhead, then press [ to set IN or ] to set OUT there.
  5. Changed your mind? Use the undo and redo buttons, or the reset button to select the whole track again.

Shortcuts pause while you type. The space bar and bracket keys are ignored when a text box or the format menu has focus. Click an empty part of the page to use them again.

If something isn’t working

My file won’t load

Your browser does the decoding, so support depends on the browser. MP3 and WAV work almost everywhere. If an M4A, OGG or FLAC file fails, convert it to WAV or MP3 and try again. Very long or very large files can also run out of memory, and a video with no sound track has nothing to extract.

Getting audio from a video takes a long time

When a browser can’t read the video directly, it has to play the whole video to record its sound. That takes about as long as the video itself. MP4 and WEBM files are the most likely to open quickly.

The MP3 export failed

The MP3 encoder is fetched the first time you export an MP3. If it can’t load, for example because the connection dropped, export as WAV instead, or reload the page and try again.

The clip has a click at the start or end

A cut in the middle of a sound can leave a sudden jump. Tick Fade in / out on export to smooth the first and last 150 ms.

Where did my exported file go?

It’s saved by your browser as a normal download, usually in your Downloads folder, named after the original file with -trimmed added. Some browsers ask where to save it first.
